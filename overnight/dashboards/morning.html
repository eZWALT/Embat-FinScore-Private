The question

The brief gives two pictures: Northbrook Foods recovering from 45 to 65 and Velasco Industrial sliding from 82 to 68. In the last month they can look alike. So the object is the trajectory, with a reason and with how early it showed. Judges will score companies we have never seen, so we froze 72 companies at the start and never touched them.

How to read the numbers

Ranking score. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. Read 0.76 as: pick one company-month that later recovered and one that did not; the model puts the recovering one first 76 times in 100.

Two bars every signal must clear. Company size alone already ranks recovery at 0.617. The number of days with any bank movement (“the activity clock”) already ranks it at 0.711. For every candidate we strip out what the activity clock explains and measure what is left over; under 0.55 means nothing new. A candidate also has to beat size by at least 0.02, and must not be a near-copy (correlation above 0.80) of something we already keep.

Dark companies. 470 of the 1,214 have no invoices connected. Invoice-based readings stay blank for them, never zero.

The story of the night

The first overnight run reported a ranking score of 0.86. It predicted a company’s future cash from its own cash columns — target and input were the same thing. We erased it and fixed one rule: the outcome we predict may never be built from the columns the model is allowed to see.

We then built the trail (118 measurements per company per month from all eight tables), defined nine yes/no outcomes with rules taken from the credit literature, and audited roughly forty candidate signals one by one. Two readings survived every check. Everything else was size in disguise, a twin of the activity clock, or a stable company trait rather than monthly news. That is the finding.

Reading 1 — quiet stress recovers (the 45 → 65 engine)

What it predicts. Among companies in cash stress now (negative cash, or under one month of runway), which ones will be back to three months of runway within half a year. It happens in 7% of stressed months.

What it says. The stressed months that recover are the quiet ones. Every reason points the same way: less payroll-type outflow and less activity, more likely to recover. That is de-escalation at the bottom — and partly mean reversion, which we say out loud.

Reasons we will not give: transaction count (a twin of movement days), operating inflows (company size), transfers and outflow volatility (stable traits), days sales outstanding and debt-service ratio (add nothing once activity is removed).

Reading 2 — the big customer goes quiet (dip vs fall)

What it predicts. Whether the customer who billed the most in the last quarter bills nothing in the next quarter. It happens in 29% of company-months with an invoice book.

What it says. The lead is last month’s invoicing volume and how jumpy it has been, plus credit-note share and the financing-cost ratio three months back. Days sales outstanding — how long customers take to pay — is deliberately off the card: it ranked first in a naive model and was worse than a coin flip on the companies with the shortest collection times. Removing it lifted the weakest fold from 0.556 to 0.680.

A late result this morning: whether the top customer was billed at all this month is a strong one-month lead (leftover 0.789). When last quarter’s top customer received no invoice this month, 58% are gone next quarter versus 8% when they were still billed. That is a reason we can show; it does not replace the card because it is nearly the outcome itself, one month early.

What we set aside, grouped

  • Twins of the activity clock — transaction count, gap regularity, recency, change in movement days.
  • Company size in disguise — operating inflows and outflows, number of suppliers or customers.
  • Stable traits, not monthly news — own-account transfers, outflow volatility, uncategorised share, pending-invoice share.
  • Connection artefacts — number of accounts, product flags, opening dates. They only ever go up as accounts are connected.
  • Invoice rewrites — DSO, DPO, open receivables/payables, this month’s issuance, debt-service and finance-cost amounts.
  • Snapshots we cannot use — credit-line utilisation exists for one month only (1.6% coverage).

What we will not claim

A 0–100 index or pillar weights (team decision on top of this). A score on the hidden 72 as proof (too few events). A lead longer than one month on the hidden set. An explanation of 82 → 68 without the cash columns. Reasons taken from importance plots that failed the leftover test. Bankruptcy or default language — this is a health reading.
