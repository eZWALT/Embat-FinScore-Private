## What FICO is (facts from public sources)

FICO is not one secret formula: it is a statistical scorecard.

- **5 weighted categories:** payment history 35%, amounts owed / utilization 30%, length of history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. These are FICO's published, approximate weights, not the exact formula.
- **Trained on an outcome:** the original FICO (1989) is a logistic regression trained on millions of anonymized credit files to predict serious delinquency (90+ days) within 24 months. Points per variable come out of that model, not from hand-set rules.
- **Segmented scorecards:** the US FICO uses a dozen+ scorecards, one per population type (e.g. files with or without prior delinquency).
- **Trended data:** FICO 10 T looks at up to 24 months of balances and payments. It separates "transactors" (pay the card off each month) from "revolvers" (carry balances) and penalizes rising balances. FICO claims up to ~10% fewer defaults than FICO 8 (its non-trended version).
- **Reason codes:** every score ships with its top reasons. Two people with the same 680 get different reasons.
- **Variable prep:** scorecard building uses Weight of Evidence / Information Value to bin variables and measure predictive power; monotonic, explainable relationships are a design goal (Fahner, FICO / Philadelphia Fed).

## Ideas for our score (not implemented yet)

1. **Weight payment behavior more.** FICO weights paying over static solvency. Our "cobros" pillar is 20% and "liquidez" 30%; it could go up, but invoices cover only 785 of 1,286 companies.
2. **Debt utilization.** Today we only use debt service and financial cost from transactions. `debt_products` has `outstanding/granted` for lines and factoring: a new signal, but a snapshot without history.
3. **Balance trend.** `d_runway` already goes this way. Add debt direction (rising/falling) and transactor vs revolver (credit lines drawn and not repaid).
4. **Age and new credit.** Derive from `created_at` of products: recent new accounts/debt, average age of banking relationships. Caveat: `created_at` is when the product was connected, not when it was originated.
5. **Mix and diversification.** Number of banks, product types, dependence on one bank or on factoring/confirming.
6. **Segmented scorecards.** Separate thresholds by size, ERP or sector, since a global percentile compares very different companies. Closest to FICO's segmentation, low cost.
7. **Reason codes.** Our per-signal contribution table is already the equivalent. Show the top 3-4 reasons in plain language (e.g. "low cash cushion") instead of a signal list.
8. **Learn the weights.** FICO trains on real defaults; we have no outcome. If the leaderboard gives any signal, calibrate pillar weights with a simple logistic regression on the pillars. Few-parameter, monotonic pillars are easier to defend than a big model.

Suggested order: low effort (6) segment by size and (7) plain-language reasons; medium effort (2) and (4), which use tables the score does not yet use.
